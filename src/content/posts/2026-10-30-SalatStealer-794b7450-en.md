## Behaviour analysis

**11** behaviours in total, each tied to evidence; **8** are statically recoverable and **3** were obtained dynamically.

### 1. UPX self-extract body

`T1027.002` · Confidence **high** · dynamic

The sample is UPX-packed (UPX0/UPX1/UPX2 sections; UPX1 entropy 8.0, writable and executable); at runtime it decompresses an approximately 6.5MB 64-bit Rust body in memory, and the UPX1 section entropy drops to 6.29 after unpacking.

> **Evidence**: section UPX1 entropy 8.0 -> 6.29; packed entry_point RVA 0x63c0f0; memory image sha256 bb74d808d59eaa1bad4b6594508bd35dab963e88990f08592bf5f3582b0dce2e
> 
> **Source**: `ev-000004` · `ref:0c770422bf` · `ref:8ad3e802c7` · `ref:535e8183d0`

### 2. Covert DoH resolution of C2 domains

`T1071.004` · Confidence **high** · dynamic

Immediately after start-up it sends DNS-over-HTTPS queries to 1.1.1[.]1, 8.8.8[.]8 and dns.google with the fixed header User-Agent: salat/2.0, resolving panel domains such as morkovka[.]cn and sa1atik[.]cn in turn, bypassing local DNS.

> **Evidence**: RVA 0x4d03fc "User-Agent: salat/2.0"; dynamic observation GET hxxps://1.1.1[.]1/dns-query?name=morkovka[.]cn&type=A
> 
> **Source**: `ref:816059f636` · `ref:3490665ed5` · `ev-000025`

### 3. Encrypted multi-panel exfiltration of host information

`T1071.001` · Confidence **high** · dynamic

Repeatedly POSTs 473 bytes of ciphertext as application/json to hxxps://morkovka[.]cn/qqkb/ and hxxps://sa1atik[.]cn/qqkb/, with a base64-encoded encrypted body, while keeping TCP/443 and QUIC (UDP/443) connections to C2.

> **Evidence**: dynamic POST /qqkb/ (Host: morkovka[.]cn / sa1atik[.]cn, Content-Length: 473); in-memory panel triple at RVA 0x1cfe9e
> 
> **Source**: `ref:857ba03ebd` · `ref:8ec82d2d3d` · `ev-000025` · `ev-000026`

### 4. Steal browser and wallet data

`T1555.003` · Confidence **high** · static

Enumerates Chrome/Edge/Brave/Opera/Firefox profile directories, harvests login credentials, cookies and Local State keys, and packages data from extensions/clients such as MetaMask, Phantom, TronLink, Exodus, Atomic, Telegram, Steam and Discord for exfiltration.

> **Evidence**: wallet extension IDs and browser path strings from RVA 0x4add1a; RVA 0x4a3ca6 "[steam]/[roblox]/[crypto] zipping wallets"; modules src/makelog/invdump/{chrome,gecko,steam,crypto,telegram}.rs
> 
> **Source**: `ev-000014` · `ev-000026`

### 5. Hook keyboard input

`T1056.001` · Confidence **high** · static

Installs a global keyboard hook via SetWindowsHookExW and collects keystrokes with GetAsyncKeyState/GetKeyboardState, using a separate child process keytest-quit-helper to record input.

> **Evidence**: import SetWindowsHookExW; key-name mapping table at RVA 0x4d2a02; string keytest-quit-helper (near RVA 0x4d2a02)
> 
> **Source**: `ref:af39cc896c` · `ref:595612b495`

### 6. Screen, camera and microphone capture

`T1113` · Confidence **high** · static

Uses GDI (DC/BitBlt/GetDIBits) and DXGI/Media Foundation to capture screen and camera frames, and WASAPI to capture microphone audio, supporting H.264 hardware encoding before exfiltration.

> **Evidence**: imports GetDC/BitBlt/CreateCompatibleBitmap/MFEnumDeviceSources; RVA 0x4a34d8 "cpal_wasapi_in"; modules src/screen.rs, src/dxgi_capture.rs, src/camera.rs, src/sound.rs
> 
> **Source**: `ref:af39cc896c` · `ev-000026`

### 7. Run-key persistence

`T1547.001` · Confidence **high** · static

Writes a value named opus-runscript under Software\Microsoft\Windows\CurrentVersion\Run pointing at powershell.exe, to relaunch at user logon.

> **Evidence**: RVA 0x4d25ae "opus-runscript" + RVA 0x4d25d2 "SOFTWARE\Microsoft\Windows\CurrentVersion\Run"
> 
> **Source**: `ref:595612b495` · `ev-000026`

### 8. Install itself disguised as a system process

### 9. Disable defences and add exclusions

An embedded PowerShell script calls Add-MpPreference to add Program Files, %APPDATA% and %LOCALAPPDATA% to Defender exclusion paths, suppressing detection.

### 10. Token theft and elevated execution

`T1134.001` · Confidence **high** · static

Enables privileges such as SeDebugPrivilege/SeImpersonatePrivilege/SeAssignPrimaryTokenPrivilege, uses OpenProcessToken/DuplicateTokenEx/ImpersonateLoggedOnUser and CreateProcessAsUserW to launch child processes in the target session/a higher-privilege context, and includes an scm_bypass elevation path.

> **Evidence**: imports AdjustTokenPrivileges/LookupPrivilegeValueW/ImpersonateLoggedOnUser/CreateProcessAsUserW; privilege list and scm_bypass from RVA 0x4d2500
> 
> **Source**: `ref:af39cc896c` · `ref:595612b495`

### 11. Tunnelling and remote desktop

`T1090` · Confidence **medium** · static

Embeds a SOCKS5/tunnel proxy and QUIC (h3)/WebSocket transports, plus a complete RDP client module (CredSSP/NLA, dynvc, gfx), usable as a pivot and remote-control channel.

> **Evidence**: RVA 0x4a75f0 "socks5 read timed out"; modules src/tunnel.rs, src/proxy.rs, rdp_child/{credssp,mcs,dynvc,gfx}.rs
> 
> **Source**: `ev-000026` · `ref:3490665ed5`

## Do not misjudge

:::important
The following conclusions are **easy to misjudge**; keep them in mind when citing this report.

- Do not classify as ransomware: although AES-GCM/ChaCha20/SHA-256 constants are present, they serve report encryption and hashing; no bulk user-file encryption or ransom note was seen.
- Do not classify as a downloader/dropper: no download-and-execute of a second payload was observed; the sample itself is the complete stealer/RAT body.
- Do not claim a specific vulnerability (e.g. a CVE) was exploited for elevation: the visible methods are token theft and SCM/UAC bypass, not memory-corruption exploitation.
- Do not treat all five panel domains as verified operational: only morkovka[.]cn and sa1atik[.]cn actually received reports in this observation.
:::

## Analysis method and limitations

**Limitations and pending verification**

1. Only 2 panels received actual POSTs in this observation; the other 3 come from the runtime-decrypted memory config and did not trigger.
2. The report body is encrypted binary (473 bytes); the cleartext machine information and keys were not obtained, and campaign_id is unknown.
3. Installation to %APPDATA%\RuntimeBroker.exe and Run-key persistence come from code and embedded-config evidence; this run did not perform per-item registry/filesystem forensics.
4. The remote-control command stream after session establishment was not captured (the observation window was dominated by reporting and connection maintenance).
